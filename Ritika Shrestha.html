Live simulation

Who gets the next slot?

One base station, five users at different distances, one user served per time slot. Three classic rules answer differently. In my paper I trained a Random Forest to imitate Proportional Fair, using Monte Carlo data. Change the settings and compare the three rules on the same random channel.

Seed 42
Shades run from user 1 (nearest, strongest channel) to user 5 (farthest)

Channel model from the paper: SNR = U(0, 20) × q, with q = 1.0, 0.7, 0.4, 0.2, 0.1, Shannon rate log2(1 + SNR), and an average-rate memory of 0.9 for Proportional Fair. The Random Forest is not re-run here. Its numbers are the held-out test results reported in the paper (4,000 slots, 5 users). Live runs differ a little from the paper because each draw is a different random channel.

Rules, as in the system: hazard = Σ(component score × weight). Population score = min(100, population ÷ 10,000 × 100). Distance score = max(0, min(100, (30 − km) ÷ 30 × 100)). Exposure = 0.6 × population + 0.4 × distance. Combined risk = hazard × exposure ÷ 100. The weights and thresholds are illustrative prototype values, not calibrated for any real lake. All data in the prototype is simulated.
